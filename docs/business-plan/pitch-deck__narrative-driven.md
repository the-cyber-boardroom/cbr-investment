2. **Slide 2: Problem – Cyber Risks Lost in Translation**  
   - **Complex Threats, Unprepared Boards:** Cybersecurity is now a top business risk, but most board directors lack the background to grasp technical reports. Crucial information is “lost in translation” between security teams and the board.  
   - **Knowledge & Communication Gap:** Security officers (CISOs) struggle to explain cyber threats in business terms, and board members struggle to ask informed questions. *Result: Decisions about cyber risk are made without full understanding.* According to industry experts, boards often fail to challenge management on cyber issues due to limited knowledge ([Boards need to brush up on cybersecurity governance, survey finds | Cybersecurity Dive](https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/board-cyber-security-skills-training/711581/#:~:text=In%20organizations%20with%20less%20cyber,state%20funded%20organizations%20and%20charities)) ([Boards need to brush up on cybersecurity governance, survey finds | Cybersecurity Dive](https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/board-cyber-security-skills-training/711581/#:~:text=Clyde%20likens%20it%20to%20board,whether%20they%20are%20a%20CFO)).  
   - **Evidence of the Gap:** Nearly **60% of board members** surveyed felt they did not receive enough cybersecurity training in the last year ([Boards need to brush up on cybersecurity governance, survey finds | Cybersecurity Dive](https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/board-cyber-security-skills-training/711581/#:~:text=A%20joint%20Corporate%20Governance%20Institute,in%20the%20last%2012%20months)). Only 41% of directors say their board even moderately understands their company’s cyber info ([How to Communicate IT Security to the Board: 7 Best Practices](https://www.syteca.com/en/blog/how-to-effectively-communicate-it-security-to-the-executive-board#:~:text=Practices%20www,their%20board%20members%20understand)). This leaves companies vulnerable and boards anxious.  
   - **Real Consequences:** Inadequate board oversight can lead to missed warning signs of breaches, slower response to incidents, and regulatory compliance failures. (E.g., high-profile breaches have led to lawsuits implicating boards for negligence.) Boards know they *must* improve their cyber literacy – but how?

3. **Slide 3: Why Now – Growing Threats & New Regulations**  
   - **Escalating Cyber Threat Landscape:** Cyber attacks are increasing in frequency and sophistication. Ransomware, data breaches, and supply-chain attacks are hitting companies of all sizes. Board members, as high-profile individuals, are **12× more likely to be targeted** by cyberattacks like spear-phishing ([Board Members Cause Cybersecurity Gaps - Protect Their Online ...](https://blackcloak.io/board-members-cause-cybersecurity-gaps-why-cisos-must-protect-their-online-privacy/#:~:text=Board%20Members%20Cause%20Cybersecurity%20Gaps,to%20be%20targeted%20in)) – making their personal cybersecurity acumen even more crucial.  
   - **Regulatory Pressure:** Regulators have taken notice. The U.S. SEC’s new cyber disclosure rules (2023) require public company boards to report on their cybersecurity oversight and expertise ([Boards need to brush up on cybersecurity governance, survey finds | Cybersecurity Dive](https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/board-cyber-security-skills-training/711581/#:~:text=Board%20cybersecurity%20preparedness%20is%20taking,cyber%20incidents%20are%20growing%2C%20too)). Globally, guidelines are emerging for boards to assume greater responsibility for cyber risk management. **Boards without a clear cyber oversight process face compliance risks** and potential liability.  
   - **Market Awareness:** High-profile incidents and media coverage have made “cybersecurity” a standing item in board agendas. However, traditional solutions (annual workshops, dense reports) aren’t keeping pace. There is a timely **demand for innovative tools** to help boards fulfill these duties effectively.  
   - **The Opportunity:** Organizations are actively looking for ways to educate and inform their directors on cyber risk. Boards that demonstrate strong cyber oversight can not only avoid penalties but also **build trust with investors and customers**. This is the perfect time to introduce a solution that makes board-level cybersecurity engagement easier, continuous, and effective.

4. **Slide 4: Solution Overview – The Cyber Boardroom Platform**  
   - **What It Is:** The Cyber Boardroom is a **generative AI-powered platform** that acts as a *“cybersecurity advisor”* for boards of directors ([Interview: Dinis Cruz on The Cyber Boardoom | SC Media UK](https://insight.scmagazineuk.com/interview-dinis-cruz-on-the-cyber-boardoom#:~:text=UK%20insight,GenAI%29%20to%20help%20boards)). It bridges the communication gap by delivering complex cybersecurity information in a format that board members can easily understand and act on.  
   - **Core Components:** Our platform comprises interactive AI-driven tools, including:  
     - **“Athena” AI Chatbot Advisor:** An intelligent assistant that answers board members’ questions on cybersecurity in real time, in plain English. Athena provides on-demand explanations, definitions, and insights tailored to the user’s context (industry, company, prior knowledge).  
     - **Custom Cyber Brief Generator:** Automatically transforms technical security reports and metrics from the company’s IT/CISO team into **concise, relevant board briefs**. These briefings highlight what the board needs to know — in business impact terms — cutting through jargon.  
     - **Learning Modules & Updates:** Ongoing micro-learning modules for directors (e.g., a 5-minute digest on the latest cyber threat trends, or a quick quiz) to continuously build their knowledge. The platform pushes relevant updates so the board is never in the dark about emerging risks.  
   - **How It Works:** Our AI ingests both global cybersecurity knowledge and organization-specific inputs. It then uses large language models (LLMs) and proprietary algorithms to “translate” that into executive-friendly outputs. *Think of it as having a personal cyber mentor for each board member.*

7. **Slide 7: Competitive Landscape**  
   - **Status Quo Solutions:** Today, boards rely on a patchwork of solutions: annual training workshops, static slide decks from IT, PDFs, or at best, generic board portal software (e.g., Diligent, BoardEffect) that secure documents but **don’t teach or inform**. Some companies hire consultants to brief their boards or use in-house sessions – these are expensive, infrequent, and not personalized.  
   - **Emerging Players:** To our knowledge, few if any direct competitors offer an AI-driven, board-focused cybersecurity platform. There are adjacent players: for example, cybersecurity rating services (SecurityScorecard, BitSight) provide risk scores to executives, and some firms like PwC offer board cyber training modules. However, those either **lack interactivity** or are one-time services.  
   - **Our Differentiation:** **The Cyber Boardroom uniquely combines AI technology with domain expertise** to provide an *interactive, ongoing solution.* We’re not just giving a score or a report – we engage directors in dialogue and learning. Our use of generative AI (with a curated knowledge base) is a novel approach that others haven’t matched. We also emphasize context: the advice and content adapt to each company’s situation, unlike one-size-fits-all training videos or articles.  
   - **Barriers to Entry:** Our advantage compounds over time – as our AI interacts with boards, it continuously improves and accumulates a proprietary dataset of what boards care about, which new entrants would lack. Additionally, credibility is key in this space: our team’s reputation in cybersecurity makes companies more comfortable trusting an AI advisor. A newcomer without that trust or data would find it hard to catch up.

9. **Slide 9: Business Model & Revenue**  
   - **Subscription Model:** Annual subscription licensing per company. Pricing will scale with company size/board size. For instance, a mid-size firm might pay **£25k/year** for up to 10 users, while a large enterprise might pay **£50-100k/year** for enterprise-wide access and custom integrations. This model ensures recurring revenue.  
   - **Pricing Rationale:** These price points are a fraction of the cost of a single major cybersecurity incident or even a one-day board consulting workshop. We provide year-round value (continuous education and risk reduction). Our customers see this as an insurance-like investment in good governance.  
   - **Gross Margins:** As a SaaS platform, costs are mainly in development and support. Content updates and AI processing have low marginal cost. We expect gross margins ~80% at scale. There is no physical product or on-site training labor, which is a huge advantage over traditional solutions.  
   - **Upsell Opportunities:** In addition to base subscriptions, we plan to offer premium add-ons: for example, deeper integration with a company’s internal risk dashboards, or custom AI tuning to their specific industry threats. We could also offer **expert consulting hours** as a package (e.g., an annual virtual session with a renowned cyber expert for the board) – either directly or via partners – as an upsell for additional revenue.  
   - **Long-Term Revenue Streams:** Beyond enterprise clients, there’s potential to develop **an individual board member offering** (a personalized version of Athena for professional directors, perhaps via a membership model) and even an anonymized industry benchmarking report (data-driven insights on how engaged boards are across industries). These are future considerations that show the extensibility of our platform and monetization routes.
